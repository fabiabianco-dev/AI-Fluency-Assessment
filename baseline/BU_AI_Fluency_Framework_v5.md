## What This Is

This framework defines what AI Fluency means at BetterUp — not as a list of tools, not as a course catalog, but as a set of observable capabilities that any employee can develop and demonstrate.

It does three things:
1. **Defines** what AI fluency looks like in practice — using RANGE, one single behavioral measurement language across all BU AI programs
2. **Maps** where any employee can self-assess across 5 RANGE dimensions
3. **Connects** gaps to specific programs and resources

This is not a performance review tool; It is a development map for individuals.

---

## What AI Fluency Is (and Isn't)

**AI Fluency at BetterUp** is the ability to work with AI as a genuine collaborator — knowing when to bring it in, how to direct it clearly, how to evaluate what it gives you, and how to take full ownership of what you do with it.

AI Fluency is **not**:
- Knowing the most tools
- Completing the most courses
- Being the fastest person in the room

AI Fluency **is**:
- Using AI in ways that produce meaningfully better outcomes
- Iterating based on what you learn — not just repeating what worked once
- Taking responsibility for every output AI helps you produce
- Building habits and systems, not one-off shortcuts

> **The master signal of AI fluency is iteration.** Someone who tries, evaluates, adjusts, and tries again is more fluent than someone who gets lucky once. Research shows that 85.7% of effective AI interactions involve multiple refinement cycles. If your prompts never change, your fluency isn't growing.

## Three Ways AI Shows Up in Your Work

You don't graduate from one mode to another — all three exist at every level. What changes is the complexity and stakes of the tasks you apply them to.

| Practice | What It Means | Example |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Automate** | AI completes a task on your behalf. You define it; AI executes it. | AI drafts a weekly recap from your meeting notes |
| **Augment** | You and AI work side by side. You stay in the driver's seat. | You write a proposal; AI stress-tests your logic and surfaces gaps |
| **Agentic** | AI acts more independently within guardrails you define. You review outcomes. | AI monitors a pipeline and flags anomalies; you decide what to do |

A Pre-Pilot employee automates small tasks. A Multiplier uses Agentic mode for complex, high-stakes systems. But all three modes exist for everyone — the difference is fluency, scope, stakes, and oversight.

## Four Proficiency Levels

### Pre-Pilot — "I've started, but my work hasn't really changed yet."
AI is a convenience. Usage is ad hoc. You can't point to clear evidence that your output is meaningfully different as a result of AI. You're building awareness and early habits.

*Watch for:* One-off prompts. Unedited outputs. No before/after story. Uncertainty about which tool to use and why.

---

### Pilot — "I use AI to operate at a meaningfully higher level."
You have a structured, repeatable approach. You can demonstrate your AI workflow and explain where you trust it and where you don't. Your output is concretely better — not just faster.

*The test:* Can you tell your AI growth story across three points in time — what you started with, what changed, and why?

---

### Builder — "I orchestrate AI and build things others depend on."
You build reusable systems. Your team stopped doing something manually because of what you built. You treat your AI setup like a product — it has versions, it gets updated, and others use it. You have measurable before/after impact you can show.

*The test:* If you disappeared tomorrow, would what you built still be running? Do 2+ people depend on it?

---

### Multiplier — "I reshape how work happens — not just how fast it moves."
You've changed what work exists. Categories of tasks no longer require human effort. You've influenced how the broader organization operates. AI handles execution; you own judgment, strategy, and design.

*The test:* Can you point to something your team or org stopped doing entirely? Have others adopted a way of working you introduced?

---

## How This Is Not Linear

You can be at Pilot in Navigation and Pre-Pilot in Autonomy. You can be at Builder in Execution Fidelity and Applying in Reach. This is not a problem — it is the point. The framework exists to help you see *where* the gaps are, not to rank you against a ladder.

**Your level in any dimension is determined by behavioral evidence — what you actually do and can demonstrate, not what you've completed.**

## The RANGE Framework: Your Fluency Map

RANGE is the single behavioral measurement language for AI fluency at BetterUp. It is used in AFS Next to measure individual growth — and here to map where you stand across five dimensions. Each dimension contains specific sub-competencies drawn from the 6 fluency domains.

```
[ AUTOMATE ]  [ AUGMENT ]  [ AGENTIC ]
         ↑         ↑         ↑
  ——————————— RANGE ——————————————
  R · Reach       (Experimentation + Adaptation)
  A · Autonomy    (Workflow Design)
  N · Navigation  (Intent + Judgment)
  G · Generalization  (Peer Enablement)
  E · Execution Fidelity  (Voice + Ownership)
```

You have a RANGE profile — five scores, not one. Develop deliberately based on where your gaps are and what your role demands.

---

## R — Reach
*Willingness to push AI into unfamiliar territory and expand the scope of problems you bring to it*

**Sub-competencies from the 6 Domains:**
- **Experimentation Courage** — You try new approaches without waiting for permission or perfection
- **Continuous Adaptation** — You update your skills and mental models as AI evolves; your practice six months ago looks different from today

Reach is about the slope, not the snapshot. An employee who is improving steadily is more valuable than one who plateaued early, regardless of current level.

| Level | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Pre-Pilot** | Shifts from resistance or indifference to curiosity; experiments with AI even when uncertain; willing to fail visibly in service of learning; experiments are isolated to familiar tasks |
| **Pilot** | Actively stretches into unfamiliar use cases; can name what they tried, what failed, and what they learned from it; shares experiments with peers without being prompted |
| **Builder** | Runs structured experiments across team workflows; treats new AI capabilities as hypotheses to test; documents what works and what doesn't for others; pushes into Agentic territory deliberately |
| **Multiplier** | Sponsors cross-functional experimentation; shapes organizational appetite for AI risk; creates conditions for others to experiment at scale; brings new AI capabilities to BetterUp before they're obvious |

---

## A — Autonomy
*Completing AI work end-to-end without needing help — and building systems that run the same way*

**Sub-competency from the 6 Domains:**
- **Workflow Design** — You design, improve, and scale how work gets done for yourself and for others; you build systems that outlast individual conversations

Autonomy is the difference between using AI and building with it. At high Autonomy, you don't just use AI to complete tasks — you've systematized how AI fits into your operation.

| Level | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Pre-Pilot** | Applies AI to individual tasks; depends on prompts from others or tutorials; workflow is ad hoc; begins noticing where things are slower or more painful than they need to be |
| **Pilot** | Runs full AI workflows independently, start to finish; redesigns personal workflows for measurable, demonstrable gains; can show the before/after; begins documenting for reuse |
| **Builder** | Builds AI-enhanced workflows others can adopt; deploys across teams; measures adoption and impact beyond themselves; systems have versions and owners; passes the Still Running Test (2+ active users, runs without builder present) |
| **Multiplier** | Defines AI workflow standards at organizational scale; shapes infrastructure and tooling decisions; changes what categories of work exist; autonomy is organizational, not just personal |

---

## N — Navigation
*Orienting in ambiguity — knowing what to delegate to AI, when to trust it, and when not to*

**Sub-competencies from the 6 Domains:**
- **Intent** *(from Domain 1: Intent)* — Before you open a tool, you understand what you're trying to accomplish, whether AI belongs in it, and how to divide the work deliberately
- **Judgment** *(from Domain 3: Judgment)* — After AI gives you something, you apply the right level of scrutiny — to the output, to how AI reached it, and to how the collaboration is serving your goals

Navigation is the flight instrument check. It's knowing where you're going before you fly and reading the instruments while you're in the air.

### N1 — Intent (Know Before You Fly)

**Sub-skills:**
- **Goal Clarity** — You understand what you need before involving AI
- **Tool Awareness** — You know what different AI systems can and can't do, and choose accordingly
- **Work Division** — You deliberately decide what you'll own, what AI will own, and where you'll work together

| Level | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Pre-Pilot** | Identifies tasks where AI might help; begins experimenting with basic delegation; sometimes turns to AI by default without asking whether it's the right choice |
| **Pilot** | Consistently matches tasks to appropriate tools and can explain the decision; articulates clear before/after; knows when NOT to use AI for a given task |
| **Builder** | Maps AI opportunities across team workflows; helps others identify where delegation creates leverage; documents decision frameworks for tool selection |
| **Multiplier** | Shapes organizational boundaries around human vs. AI work; advises on AI scope at the strategy level; helps the org understand where AI creates risk vs. leverage |

### N2 — Judgment (Read the Instruments)

**Sub-skills:**
- **Output Quality** — You assess accuracy, relevance, coherence, and appropriateness before acting
- **Reasoning Review** — You examine how AI reached its conclusion, not just what it said
- **Interaction Quality** — You notice when AI's behavior isn't serving your goals and adjust the collaboration

| Level | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Pre-Pilot** | Doesn't blindly accept outputs; asks "does this make sense?"; beginning to develop intuition for when something is off |
| **Pilot** | Uses structured criteria to evaluate outputs; catches hallucinations, gaps, and logical errors; distinguishes between AI overconfidence and genuine accuracy |
| **Builder** | Builds QA frameworks for team use; documents where AI consistently fails vs. excels; trains others to evaluate AI outputs at the appropriate level of scrutiny |
| **Multiplier** | Defines quality standards for AI-assisted work across the org; shapes policy around verification and review; sets expectations for what "good enough" means by output type |

---

## G — Generalization
*Applying AI skills across different contexts, functions, and teams — and enabling others to develop their own*

**Sub-competency from the 6 Domains:**
- **Peer Enablement** *(from Domain 6: Growth & Community)* — You actively contribute to others' fluency — not just when asked; your skills travel beyond your own work

Generalization is how individual fluency becomes organizational capability. At Builder and Multiplier levels, this is the force multiplication dimension — your value isn't just what you can do, it's what you make possible for others.

| Level | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Pre-Pilot** | AI skills are largely task-specific and don't transfer easily; begins noticing how skills might apply in adjacent areas; learning from peers |
| **Pilot** | Shares prompts, use cases, and learnings with peers proactively; models good habits; participates in team AI rituals; begins applying skills across different task types |
| **Builder** | Runs enablement sessions; builds libraries, playbooks, or onboarding resources for team use; coaches others through resistance and complexity; demonstrates 3+ active adopters of their systems |
| **Multiplier** | Shapes culture and organizational narrative around AI; sponsors cross-functional learning programs; creates conditions for others to grow at scale; fluency spreads through their influence, not just their instruction |

---

## E — Execution Fidelity
*Consistently producing quality AI-assisted outcomes — through precise direction and full accountability for results*

**Sub-competencies from the 6 Domains:**
- **Voice** *(from Domain 2: Voice)* — Useful AI output isn't luck — it's the result of clear communication; this is how well you can direct AI toward what you actually need
- **Ownership** *(from Domain 4: Ownership)* — Whatever AI helps you create, you own it fully; this covers the ethical, professional, and operational responsibility that comes with AI-assisted work

Execution Fidelity has two sides: how clearly you brief AI (Voice) and how fully you own what comes out (Ownership). High-fidelity execution means neither side is weak.

### E1 — Voice (Brief AI Like a Contractor, Not a Mind Reader)

**Sub-skills:**
- **Output Clarity** — You define what you want: format, audience, tone, scope, constraints
- **Process Direction** — You guide how AI approaches the task, not just what to produce
- **Collaboration Setup** — You establish how you want AI to behave: concise or exploratory, directive or Socratic

| Level | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Pre-Pilot** | Writes basic prompts; beginning to learn that context and specificity improve results; often treats AI like a search engine |
| **Pilot** | Designs structured, multi-part prompts with clear specs for output and process; refines based on results; can explain what changed between Prompt v1 and v3 and why |
| **Builder** | Builds reusable prompt templates and libraries for team use; documents prompting systems; coaches others on how to get better results |
| **Multiplier** | Defines organizational prompting standards; creates systems and frameworks others use across functions; shapes how BetterUp communicates with AI at scale |

### E2 — Ownership (The Pilot Is Always Responsible)

**Sub-skills:**
- **Thoughtful Selection** — You choose AI tools deliberately, based on the task's requirements and stakes
- **Transparency** — You're honest about AI's role with the people who need to know
- **Verification Before Sharing** — You verify before you publish, present, distribute, or act on AI-assisted output

| Level | What it looks like |
|-------|--------------------|
| **Pre-Pilot** | Understands that AI use comes with responsibility; learning BetterUp's disclosure expectations; building habits around verification |
| **Pilot** | Consistently verifies outputs before acting on or sharing them; transparent about AI's role in deliverables; makes conscious tool choices based on the stakes involved |
| **Builder** | Coaches others on responsible use; builds team norms, guidelines, and review processes; raises concerns when AI is being used in ways that create risk |
| **Multiplier** | Shapes organizational policy around responsible AI use; defines ethical guardrails at scale; serves as a governance resource across functions |

## What Separates the Levels: The Artifact Test

One of the clearest signals of AI fluency is whether you build **artifacts** — things that outlast the conversation and that others use. But there's a trap: more experienced users build more artifacts but often *evaluate* them less. As you progress, the discipline of evaluation matters as much as the act of creation.

| Level | Artifact Behavior |
|-------|------------------|
| Pre-Pilot | Produces outputs in conversation; rarely saves or reuses |
| Pilot | Saves and reuses prompts; documents personal workflows |
| Builder | Creates artifacts specifically for others' use; maintains and versions them |
| Multiplier | Creates systems of artifacts; others create artifacts within their frameworks |

## Your RANGE Profile → Learning Path

After self-assessing, you have a profile across 5 dimensions. Use this routing logic to find the right next step.

**If you're Pre-Pilot across most dimensions:**
→ Your Fluency Score will recommend a Coursera GenAI learning path matched to your dimension gaps. Paths are curated by function and proficiency level — start there, build habits, and re-assess once your usage data shows consistent engagement (20+ days).

**If you're Pre-Pilot in Reach (R) specifically:**
→ The block is mindset, not skill. Peer community, manager modeling, and low-stakes experimentation are the right first moves. Your Fluency Score will surface the most relevant Coursera foundational path for your function.

**If you're Pilot in most dimensions but stuck in Autonomy (A):**
→ You're ready for AFS Next. This is the primary dimension AFS Next addresses — systematizing from personal habit to team-level deployment.

**If you're Pilot in Execution Fidelity (E) but lower in Navigation (N):**
→ Role-specific Coursera tracks. Your prompting mechanics are strong but your judgment about when/how to apply AI is underdeveloped. GenAI for HR, GenAI for Sales, or equivalent functional depth tracks address this.

**If you're Builder in Autonomy (A) and Navigation (N) but Pre-Pilot in Generalization (G):**
→ You've built things but haven't multiplied them. 

**If you're approaching Builder across the board:**
→ AI Builder Change Agent. Portfolio-based. You earn it by demonstrating deployed solutions, force multiplication (3–5 active users), and quantified impact — not by completing a course.

## Role Context: What Each Level Looks Like in Practice

Fluency is right-sized. Not everyone needs to operate across all dimensions at the same depth. What counts as sufficient fluency varies by role and career trajectory.

**Individual contributor, non-technical:**
Pilot in Navigation + Execution Fidelity is the relevant standard. Autonomy at Pilot (personal workflow redesign). Reach at Pilot (active experimenter, shares learnings). Generalization at Pilot (contributes to team culture, not necessarily leading enablement).

**People manager:**
Pilot across all dimensions, with Builder in Generalization. If your team isn't growing, your own Pilot status doesn't move the needle. Managers are the single biggest unlock — or blocker — for team AI capability.

**AI Champion:**
Builder in Generalization and Reach. Pilot minimum in all others. Champions are role models — their value is modeling what fluent AI work looks like and surfacing gaps back to L&D.

**Head of function / senior leader:**
Pilot in Navigation (judgment + intent) is non-negotiable. Builder in Autonomy (you've redesigned how your function operates). Multiplier ambition in Generalization — you're changing what's possible for your function, not just your own work.

**AI Builder / Multiplier:**
Builder or Multiplier across all dimensions. Still Running Test passes. Force multiplication demonstrated. Impact quantified.

### The Gap Map

What no single program covers completely:
- **Ownership (E2)** is consistently underleveraged across all programs — requires manager modeling and embedded team norms
- **Judgment (N2) at Pilot+** requires deliberate practice in role-specific contexts, not just exposure
- **Workflow Design (A)** requires a role-specific context generic programs cannot provide — AFS Next addresses this but cohort capacity is limited
- **Generalization (G) at Builder+** must be built into team operating rhythms and community structures, not just programs
